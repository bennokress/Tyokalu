# Time Tracking of Work Projects

**Automation** · Track working hours based on context

Manually starting and stopping a time tracker is one of those things that sounds easy but gets forgotten constantly. The trick is to let your iPhone or Mac detect when you're working and handle the tracking automatically.

## What It Does

An automation that detects when you start and stop working, then logs the time - either in a simple note, a spreadsheet, a calendar event, or a dedicated time-tracking service like Toggl or Clockify.

## Detecting Work Start and Stop

The key challenge is finding a signal that reliably indicates you're actually working - not just near your desk or at the office, but genuinely started your workday. The right trigger depends on your work setup and daily routine:

- **Location-based** → Arrive at / leave the office
- **Wi-Fi-based** → Connect to / disconnect from your office network
- **App-based** → Open / close a work-specific app like Slack or your IDE
- **NFC tag** → Scan a tag at your desk to clock in and out
- **Bluetooth** → Connect to a specific device at your workspace

These are just the most common options - browse through the available automation triggers in the Shortcuts app to see if something else fits your situation better, or find a way to trigger the automation on your Mac using Shortcuts CLI. You can also combine multiple triggers for accuracy.
